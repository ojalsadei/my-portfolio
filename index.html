CASE STUDY — 2026

Sanad

A receipt-first system for keeping proof of purchase and tracking product warranties without relying on a cloud account.

Role Product Design & Development
Platform iOS / Expo
Architecture Local-first
Stack React Native · TypeScript · SQLite
01

THE PROBLEM

A warranty is only useful when you can prove the purchase.

Receipts are easy to lose. Even when they're saved, the purchase date, products and warranty information are usually scattered across photos, emails and paper copies.

Sanad brings those pieces into one structured place so users can retrieve proof of purchase and know when product coverage ends.

AT A GLANCE

Warranty status, without digging through receipts.

The home screen prioritizes products whose warranties are close to expiration while still giving an overview of all covered products.

02

PRODUCT MODEL

The receipt is the source of truth.

Sanad is receipt-centered rather than product-centered. One receipt can contain multiple products, and each product can have its own price, warranty duration and expiration date.

This keeps the digital structure close to the real-world purchase instead of duplicating receipt data for every product.

RECEIPT ARCHIVE

Purchase history in one place.

Receipts can be found by store or product, with totals and product counts visible at a glance.

RECEIPT DETAIL

Proof and products stay connected.

The original receipt remains attached to its purchase data and every product covered by it.

03

THE FLOW

Save the proof first. Add context second.

Users begin with the actual receipt: camera, photo library or PDF. They then review purchase information and attach the products included in that transaction.

Warranty expiration is derived from the purchase date and selected warranty duration rather than the date the item was added to the app.

LOCAL-FIRST ARCHITECTURE

Personal purchase data stays personal.

Sanad does not require an account or backend for its core experience. Receipt files stay on the device while structured metadata is stored locally in SQLite.

PERSONAL BY DEFAULT

The app adapts to the user, not the other way around.

Appearance, language, calendar and default currency can all be controlled independently.

Arabic does not force a Hijri calendar, and receipt currency does not modify the user's global default.

WHAT I LEARNED

Good product decisions simplify the code and the experience at the same time.

Building Sanad required thinking beyond individual screens: defining the data model, handling local files, managing navigation state, designing warranty logic and making the experience work naturally across languages.
