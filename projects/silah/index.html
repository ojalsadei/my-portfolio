Silah

Government services that start from the person, not the service name.

Silah explores a different service discovery model. Instead of expecting people to know which service they need, the system starts from their current situation, understands what changed, evaluates relevant rules and explains what may matter next.

01 Overview

A service model built around context.

Public services are often organized by institution, program or service name. That structure makes sense internally, but the person using the service may only know that something changed in their life.

Silah tests a different question: what if the system could start from the person's situation and connect them to relevant services, implications and next steps?

02 The problem

People think in situations. Systems often think in services.

A person may know that their income changed, they received a new job offer, their contract ended or their family situation changed.

They may not know the formal service name, which authority owns it or which eligibility rule should be checked first.

That gap creates unnecessary searching, repeated questions and a higher chance of missing something relevant.

Traditional starting point

“Which service are you looking for?”

The person must understand the government structure before the system can help.

Silah starting point

“Tell me what changed.”

The system interprets the situation first, then checks what may be relevant.

03 How it works

Language helps understand the question. Rules remain responsible for the decision logic.

The prototype separates conversational understanding from service evaluation. AI can help interpret language when necessary, but it does not decide eligibility or write the final service outcome.

01
Person

Natural language

A person describes a situation using normal language, including informal salary expressions and follow-up questions.

02
Local understanding

Extract clear facts

Deterministic logic identifies values such as income, job stage, negation and known service terminology before any AI call is considered.

03
Conversation context

Keep what is already known

Session variables preserve important facts so the system does not repeatedly ask the same questions.

04
AI when needed

Resolve language ambiguity

Groq is used selectively for language understanding when local logic cannot confidently resolve the phrasing.

05
Rules engine

Evaluate the service logic

A deterministic rules layer evaluates relevance, availability and known eligibility conditions using the structured facts.

06
Result

Explain what matters next

The response is built from rule results and service knowledge, with the official source remaining the final reference.

05 Decision model

“Not currently using a service” is not the same as “not eligible.”

One of the key modeling decisions was to separate service state into several independent dimensions rather than collapsing everything into one answer.

Current

Current enrollment

Is the person already using the service?

Availability

Application availability

Can the service be applied for or used in principle?

Eligibility

Known eligibility

Do we have enough verified facts to make an eligibility assessment?

Context

Relevance

Is the service relevant to the person's current situation or scenario?

06 Experience

The same idea needed to remain clear on both desktop and mobile.

The interface keeps the person's known context visible, places the conversation at the center and progressively reveals deeper analysis without making the first screen feel like a policy document.

The live prototype is responsive and preserves the same service logic across screen sizes.

08 Boundaries
Important context

A personal concept, not an official government service.

Silah is a personal prototype exploring a possible service model in the Saudi government context. It is not an official Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development product.

All personas and demo data are fictional. Service rules represented in the prototype are informed by published official sources, but the prototype does not issue official eligibility or legal decisions.

The official source remains the final reference.
